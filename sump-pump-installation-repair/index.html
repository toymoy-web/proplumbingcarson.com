Not Sure What's Wrong?

Worried About Flooding During the Rainy Season?

If your property has a crawl space, basement, or any area sitting below the surrounding grade, groundwater intrusion during Southern California's rainy season is a real risk, particularly for properties in Carson's lower-lying areas where drainage naturally directs water toward certain lots. A sump pump collects that water in a basin and pumps it away from the foundation before it accumulates.

A sump pump that isn't installed, or one that's installed but failing, is often only discovered as a problem during the first serious rain of the season, which is exactly the wrong time to find out. We install new systems and service existing ones, including battery backup options for properties where losing power during a storm would otherwise mean losing pump function too.

Signs You Need This Service

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

🔇

Pump Doesn't Run During Testing

A pump that doesn't activate when water is poured into the basin as a test has likely failed.

🔊

Unusual Noise During Operation

Grinding or rattling can indicate a failing motor or debris caught in the impeller.

🔋

No Battery Backup Installed

A pump with no backup power source will fail during the exact storm conditions most likely to cause a power outage.

💧

Visible Moisture Despite a Working Pump

Can mean the pump is undersized for the actual water volume or the basin needs attention.

In Carson, CA

Sump Pumps in Carson's Lower-Lying Areas

Carson sits at a relatively low elevation within the Los Angeles basin, and certain parts of the city, particularly older properties and those near natural drainage paths, are more prone to groundwater accumulation during the region's rainy months than higher-elevation neighborhoods nearby. A property without a sump pump in a naturally lower-lying spot is relying entirely on grading and drainage to keep water out, which isn't always sufficient during a heavier storm.

For properties that already have a sump pump, annual testing before the rainy season starts is worth doing regardless of how the system performed last year, since motors and float switches do wear out gradually. A pump that worked fine last winter isn't guaranteed to work this winter without a check.

Battery backup systems are worth serious consideration for any property where sump pump failure would mean real damage, since power outages during storms are common and a battery backup is often what actually prevents a flood in that scenario rather than the pump itself.
