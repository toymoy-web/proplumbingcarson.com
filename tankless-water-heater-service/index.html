Not Sure What's Wrong?

Tankless Water Heater Losing Performance?

A tankless unit that's taking longer to deliver hot water, showing an error code, or providing inconsistent temperature is often suffering from mineral scale buildup on its internal heat exchanger, a maintenance issue rather than a sign the unit is failing outright. Unlike a standard tank, which just needs occasional flushing, tankless units are more sensitive to scale because the heat exchanger has much tighter internal passages.

Manufacturers typically recommend descaling a tankless unit annually, more often in hard water areas, to prevent the kind of buildup that eventually forces the unit to work harder and can shorten its lifespan significantly.

In Carson, CA

Tankless Maintenance for Carson's Water

Southern California's water is moderately hard, which means mineral content builds up inside a tankless unit's heat exchanger faster than it would in a soft-water region. Without regular descaling, that buildup restricts flow through the narrow internal passages, forcing the unit to work harder and eventually reducing both efficiency and lifespan.

Descaling involves circulating a mild acidic solution through the unit's heat exchanger to dissolve mineral deposits, a process that typically takes 45 minutes to an hour and should be done annually for most Carson households, more often for larger households with higher hot water demand.

Beyond descaling, tankless units have other components, igniters, flow sensors, venting, that can fail independently of scale buildup. We diagnose the actual cause of a performance issue rather than assuming descaling will fix every problem.

If your tankless unit was installed by a different company or its service history is unclear, we can assess its current condition and establish a maintenance baseline going forward, since starting a regular descaling schedule late is still far better than not starting one at all.
