Not Sure What's Wrong?

Topping Off the Pool More Than Usual?

Southern California's warm climate means real evaporation, typically a quarter to half an inch of water loss per day in summer, but water loss beyond that points to an actual leak somewhere in the pool's shell, plumbing, or equipment. The bucket test is a simple first check: place a bucket filled to the pool's waterline on a step, mark both water levels, and compare the drop after 24 hours. If the pool loses noticeably more than the bucket, there's a real leak to find.

Pool leaks come from a handful of common points: cracks in the shell or tile line, worn seals around skimmers and return outlets, a cracked pool light housing, or a break in the underground plumbing running to the equipment pad. Finding the exact source before repairing anything is what separates a permanent fix from a repair that doesn't hold.

Signs You Need This Service

Signs Your Pool Has a Leak

🪣

Failing the Bucket Test

Losing noticeably more water than a control bucket over 24 hours points to a real leak, not evaporation.

🌱

Soggy Grass or Spongy Deck

Wet landscaping or a section of deck that feels soft or has started to shift can mean water is escaping underground nearby.

💧

Air Bubbles From Return Jets

Air in the returns often means a suction-side plumbing leak drawing air into the system.

🧪

Rising Chemical Costs

Constantly refilling with fresh water dilutes chemical balance, driving up how much you need to add to compensate.

In Carson, CA

Pool Leak Detection Around Carson

Carson has a meaningful number of single-family homes with in-ground pools, particularly in the tract-home neighborhoods built from the 1960s onward, and pool plumbing from that era is now decades past its original installation. Underground pool lines, along with skimmer and return fittings, are common failure points as the surrounding soil shifts slightly over the years and original seals age out.

We use acoustic listening equipment and pressure testing on the pool's plumbing lines to pinpoint a leak's location before recommending any repair, the same non-invasive approach used for slab leak detection in a home's main plumbing. That avoids breaking into deck or coping on a guess, which is a common and costly mistake when a leak's location isn't confirmed first.

Once located, a plumbing-line leak can often be repaired by accessing the specific point through a small, targeted cut in the deck rather than a wide excavation. Leaks in the shell itself, at the tile line, or around a light housing are typically handled with targeted patching once identified. We'll walk through which category your leak falls into once we've found it, along with a real cost estimate before any repair work starts.

What Affects the Price of Pool Leak Detection & Repair

Plumbing leak vs. shell or equipment leak

Each type needs a different detection approach and repair method, which affects overall cost.

Direct repair vs. rerouting

Rerouting an underground pool line avoids breaking the deck but adds material for the new path.

Equipment pad accessibility

An easily accessible pad costs less to inspect and service than one in a tight side-yard space.
