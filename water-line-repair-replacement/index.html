Not Sure What's Wrong?

Low Pressure or Wet Yard Near the Street? It Might Be the Service Line

The water service line runs underground from the city's connection point, usually near the street, to your home's main shutoff valve. When it fails, from corrosion, ground shifting, or tree root pressure, it typically shows up as unexpectedly low water pressure throughout the house, an unusually wet or soggy area in the yard along the line's path, or a spike in the water bill.

This is a different pipe than the interior supply lines a repipe would address, and a different repair scope than a single fixture leak. We confirm the location and cause before recommending open trenching or a trenchless approach.

Signs You Need This Service

Signs of a Water Service Line Problem

📉

Sudden Low Pressure Throughout the House

A drop affecting every fixture, not just one, often points to the incoming service line rather than interior plumbing.

🌱

Unusually Green or Wet Yard Patch

A soggy area along the line's likely path, even without recent rain, suggests a leak underground.

💵

Rising Water Bill

A leaking service line can lose a significant volume of water before any sign appears above ground.

🔊

Sound of Water Running Underground

Sometimes audible near the leak location, particularly at night when ambient noise is lower.

In Carson, CA

Service Line Issues in Carson

Older water service lines in parts of Carson were installed using galvanized steel or, in some cases, older polybutylene material, both of which are more prone to failure than the copper or modern plastic piping used in current installations. Ground shifting, tree root pressure, and decades of gradual corrosion are the most common causes of a service line failure in this kind of older infrastructure.

Trenchless replacement, pulling a new line through the ground using the existing line's path as a guide, avoids digging a full trench across a yard or under a driveway, which matters a lot for homeowners with established landscaping or hardscaping they don't want torn up. It's not always possible depending on the specific site conditions and existing line's routing, but where it is, it's usually the less disruptive and comparably priced option.

We always confirm the exact cause and extent of a service line problem before recommending open trenching or trenchless repair, since the wrong approach for a given situation can end up costing more in the long run than doing it right the first time.
