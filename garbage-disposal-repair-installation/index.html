Common Garbage Disposal Problems

Humming but Not Spinning

Garbage Disposal Service in Carson

Most garbage disposal problems fall into one of a few categories: a mechanical jam, an electrical issue, or a worn-out seal or motor at the end of the unit's service life. A disposal that's humming but not spinning can often be freed manually within minutes, while a leak from the bottom of the unit usually means the internal seal has failed and full replacement is the more practical fix.

Garbage disposals typically last 8 to 12 years with normal use. If yours is within that range and has a specific, isolated problem, a repair usually makes sense. Past that range, especially with a leak or a failing motor, replacement with a comparable or upgraded unit is generally the better value, since a repaired older unit is likely to develop a new issue again soon.

Installation includes properly connecting the disposal to both the sink drain and, where applicable, the dishwasher drain line, along with confirming the electrical connection is correct, since a miswired disposal is a common cause of units that fail prematurely.

When replacing an older disposal, upgrading to a higher horsepower model can meaningfully reduce future jams, particularly for households that regularly put fibrous scraps like celery or potato peels down the drain. We'll recommend a unit sized to your household's actual use rather than defaulting to the cheapest available model.
