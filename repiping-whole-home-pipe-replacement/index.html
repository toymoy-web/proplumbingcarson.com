Not Sure What's Wrong?

Repeated Leaks or Bad Water Pressure? Here's What It Might Mean

If you've called a plumber for a leak two or three times in the past year, in different rooms or fixtures, that pattern points to systemic pipe failure rather than bad luck. Galvanized steel supply lines, common in homes built before the 1960s, corrode from the inside over decades, narrowing the pipe and eventually failing at random points along its length.

Discolored water when you first turn on a tap, especially after the water's been sitting overnight, is another common sign, since it usually means rust is flaking off the interior of an aging galvanized line. A full repipe replaces the supply lines throughout the home with copper or PEX, both of which resist the internal corrosion that causes this pattern in older pipe.

In Carson, CA

Repiping Older Homes in Carson

Carson has neighborhoods with housing stock going back to the city's early development, and homes from that era were commonly plumbed with galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized pipe has a functional lifespan of roughly 40 to 60 years before internal corrosion becomes a serious problem, which puts a meaningful share of Carson's older housing stock at or past that point.

A whole-home repipe is a significant project, but it's usually less disruptive than most homeowners expect. We typically work room by room, cutting minimal access points in drywall, and coordinate the work to keep water shut off for the shortest time possible. Copper and PEX are both code-compliant options in California; we'll walk through the tradeoffs, copper's proven longevity versus PEX's lower cost and faster installation, based on your specific home.

Because repiping touches nearly every fixture in the home, it's also a natural point to address other issues, like replacing old shutoff valves that may no longer seal properly, at the same time rather than as a separate future project.

Pricing Transparency

What Affects the Price of Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement

We can't give an accurate number without seeing the job, but these are the real factors that move the price up or down.

Copper vs. PEX

Copper costs more upfront with a longer track record; PEX costs less and installs faster with a strong warranty history.

Number of bathrooms and fixtures

A repipe scales with how many fixtures the new lines need to reach throughout the home.

Drywall access required

Homes with more finished walls need more patching after the new lines are run.

Home's overall square footage

A larger home simply has more linear feet of pipe to replace.

Shutoff valve condition

Old valves are often replaced at the same time, since accessing them again later means opening the wall a second time.

On insurance: A repipe addresses gradual pipe deterioration, which insurers generally treat as expected wear rather than a covered event. Damage from an individual pipe failure along the way may be handled differently.
